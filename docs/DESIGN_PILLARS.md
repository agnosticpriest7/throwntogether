# Thrown Together — Design Pillars

## 1. Stay Hands-On as Long as You Want
Progression never removes the cooking game. Delegation is permission, not forced retirement from gameplay.

## 2. People Are the Automation
Progression is built around employees, skill development, staffing, delegation, promotions, and managers. Equipment expands capability; employees expand what can operate without direct player attention.

## 3. Your Restaurant Persists
Cash, inventory, equipment, staff, reputation, layout choices, menu identity, and restaurant history accumulate over time.

## 4. Growth Creates Capacity, Not Victory
Bigger is optional. Expansion creates capacity and complexity. A small elite restaurant is as legitimate as a large operation.

## 5. Build a Restaurant Company From the Floor Up
The long arc may grow from doing dishes yourself to hiring staff, managing a restaurant, and eventually operating multiple locations — while always retaining direct access to hands-on restaurant work.

## Supporting principles
- Single-player fully viable
- Couch co-op remains core
- Player-created operational pressure is better than arbitrary chaos
- Menus have operational consequences
- Inventory has financial consequences
- Reputation creates baseline demand
- Advertising creates temporary risk/reward
- Bulk purchasing creates strategic commitment
- Readability over visual noise
- Prefer simple systems that interact
