# Thrown Together — Current Game Design

## Elevator pitch
Thrown Together is a persistent restaurant-management and hands-on cooking game with optional local couch co-op.

Players begin in a tiny restaurant doing nearly everything themselves. As the restaurant succeeds, they can hire employees to take over jobs they no longer want to perform.

The long-term fantasy is:

**work the restaurant yourself → build a staff → become a working owner → manage a restaurant → open additional locations → operate a restaurant company**

At every stage, the player may still personally work a shift.

## Core activities
Players may buy ingredients, manage inventory, choose/change the menu, prep, cook, plate, serve, clear tables, wash dishes, purchase/configure equipment, hire/schedule staff, expand the kitchen/dining room, build reputation, advertise, and eventually manage multiple locations.

## Single-player and co-op
Single-player is fully supported. Local couch co-op is core but optional. No core recipe or restaurant task should require a second human player.

## Kitchen
Preferred layout is an open shared ring/circuit kitchen:
- pantry/supplies clustered to one side/corner
- central prep/staging space
- appliances around accessible edges
- clear service pickup
- logical dirty-dish/sink support area
- smooth circulation

Throwing remains useful but optional.

## Persistent restaurant
Cash, inventory, reputation, staff, equipment, layout/space upgrades, and restaurant identity persist. The original restaurant may be kept forever. Expansion is optional.

## Inventory
Ingredients are purchased. Unused stock persists. Bulk purchases reduce per-unit cost and encourage menu planning around owned inventory.

For now:
- no spoilage
- no freshness meters
- no stock decay

## Menu and recipes
Menus may change frequently. Recipes interact with ingredients, installed equipment, prep capacity, employee capability, demand, and restaurant concept.

Unlocking recipes never forces a change of restaurant type.

## Equipment and space
Kitchen space is finite. Equipment/workstations consume real capacity. Kitchen expansion increases available capacity.

Appliance upgrades should create tangible capability rather than tiny abstract stat boosts.

## Dining, reputation, and advertising
Dining capacity is finite and expandable. Persistent reputation determines baseline demand. Advertising temporarily increases demand.

Demand may exceed seating or kitchen throughput. A small high-demand restaurant is a legitimate strategy.

## Employees
Employees are a primary differentiator.

Potential roles:
- servers
- bus staff
- dishwashers
- prep cooks
- line cooks
- hosts
- supervisors
- managers

Employees should not be human-shaped conveyor belts.

Long-term employee design may include understandable role skills, strengths/weaknesses, experience gained by doing work, wages, promotions, transfers, work history, and limited personality traits.

Employees may grow into new responsibilities over time.

## Management progression
Players do everything
→ first employee
→ small staff
→ working owner
→ team management
→ restaurant manager
→ second location
→ multiple managers
→ restaurant group/company

Earlier hands-on gameplay is never removed.

## Multiple locations
Successful players may eventually open additional locations while preserving the original. Locations can share a concept or be completely different.

Experienced employees may be transferred. Managers may eventually operate locations without the player present. Off-screen locations should be simulated economically rather than rendered continuously.

The player can visit and personally work at any owned location.

## Visual direction
- simple stylized 3D
- 2.5D presentation
- fixed elevated camera
- orthographic or mild perspective
- smooth analogue movement
- grid-aware restaurant/equipment placement where useful
- clarity closer to PlateUp than Overcooked
- slightly more expressive/cute characters than PlateUp

Prototype assets may remain primitive.

## Art strategy
Use cheap placeholder/AI-assisted art during mechanical development. Once the game is strongly proven, use human specialists where they materially improve the finished product.

## Commercial direction
Target a legitimate Steam/Windows release while retaining Web builds for rapid testing. Generative AI does not need to run inside the released game.
